\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Single-cell FRET archives of bacterial chemotaxis support two different statistics of the same
cells: a population amplitude table $R(B,F)$ and a per-cell midpoint distribution $\Khalf(B)$.
A joint fit of the standard Monod--Wyman--Changeux (MWC) cluster model with methylation-based
adaptation to these two statistics was recently reported to fail, with a midpoint error of
$0.62\dex$ at the amplitude-optimal parameter set, implying that no single parameter set describes
the receptor layer. Here we show that this conclusion does not survive a consistent definition of
the midpoint. Two conventions are in use: the concentration at which a cell reaches an absolute
response threshold, and the concentration at which it reaches half of its own maximal response.
On the Moore \emph{et al.} archive ($0$--$100\,\mu$M MeAsp backgrounds, 902 cells) the two
conventions give midpoints that differ by up to $0.5\dex$ and censor $45$--$68\%$ versus
$<1\%$ of cells. We recompute the joint fit on a common footing, with the model midpoint reported
on the same total-ligand axis as the measurement. Under the half-maximal convention the standard
model reproduces both statistics with one parameter set: $R^2=0.980$ on the amplitude table with a
mean midpoint error of $0.023\dex$, and that error stays below $0.025\dex$ across the entire
amplitude-to-midpoint weighting sweep. Under the absolute-threshold convention the same
correction still admits an acceptable fit ($R^2=0.905$, $0.092\dex$), which the original
seven-point sweep missed. The reported incompatibility, and the cooperativity contradiction it
implied ($N\approx2$--$4$ versus $N\approx12$--$39$), are therefore artefacts of comparing a
relative-half-maximal model prediction against an absolute-threshold measurement on a displaced
axis. The standard model class is not ruptured by this archive. Two limits remain, and both are
reported here. The fitted cooperativity is not identifiable: the data constrain $N\gtrsim8$ and
leave a strong ridge against $K_A$, so the value $N=39$ is one point on that ridge rather than an
estimate. And the population is not homogeneous: single-cell midpoints scatter by
$0.11$--$0.26\dex$ at low background and much less at high background, and sensitivity co-varies
weakly with responsiveness (Spearman $\rho=+0.205$, $p=7\times10^{-7}$, $n=579$ saturated cells). We
state the convention dependence explicitly, because it is large enough to reverse a published
conclusion, and the residual heterogeneity explicitly, because it is what a single-parameter-set
description leaves out.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\emph{Escherichia coli} chemotaxis couples ligand binding at receptor clusters to flagellar motor
output through a short, well-mapped pathway. The receptor layer is standardly described by two-state
MWC models of receptor clusters \cite{Monod1965,SourjikBerg2002} combined with methylation-based
feedback that restores the adapted activity after a ligand step \cite{BarkaiLeibler1997}. The
framework accounts for adaptation exactness \cite{BarkaiLeibler1997}, response rescaling across
backgrounds \cite{Lazova2011}, and the input--output relation measured by in vivo FRET
\cite{SourjikBerg2002,Shimizu2010,Tu2008}.

The single-cell archive of Moore \emph{et al.} \cite{Moore2024} makes a sharper test possible. It
records FRET responses of individual cells to foreground steps $F$ delivered on seven adaptation
backgrounds $B$ from $0$ to $100\,\mu$M MeAsp, with 57 to 268 cells per background. Because the
data are single-cell, two statistics can be built from the same trajectories: the population
amplitude table $R(B,F)$, and the per-cell midpoint $\Khalf(B)$, the concentration at which a cell's
response reaches half of a reference level.

The choice of reference level is not innocuous. Two conventions appear in the literature and in
analysis code, and they are not interchangeable: \emph{(i)} an \emph{absolute} threshold, a fixed
response level such as $0.5$ in normalised FRET units, applied identically to every cell; and
\emph{(ii)} a \emph{relative} threshold, half of each cell's own maximal observed response. Under
\emph{(i)} a cell whose response never reaches the fixed level has no midpoint and is right-censored;
under \emph{(ii)} every responding cell has one. A joint fit that compares a model midpoint defined
one way against a measurement defined the other way will report a discrepancy that belongs to the
definitions rather than to the model.

This paper reports such a case. A previous analysis of this archive reported that the standard MWC
class cannot reproduce both statistics with one parameter set: at the amplitude-optimal fit the
midpoint error was $0.62\dex$, and no point of the amplitude-to-midpoint trade-off entered a
pre-specified acceptance region. We show that the model midpoint in that analysis was expressed as a
foreground increment $F$ and compared against a measurement recorded on the total-ligand axis
$B+F$, and that the measurement used an absolute threshold while the model used a relative one.
Correcting both, the standard model reproduces the two statistics with a single parameter set.

\section{Two statistics and two midpoint conventions}
\label{sec:statistics}

The Moore \emph{et al.} archive \cite{Moore2024} (Dryad doi:10.5061/dryad.nvx0k6dzz, CC0) contains
single-cell FRET trajectories for foreground steps at five levels per background, across
backgrounds $B=0,\,0.01,\,0.1,\,0.3,\,1,\,10,\,100\,\mu$M MeAsp. We analysed 24 fields of view
covering all seven backgrounds, retaining 902 cells with at least three usable stimulus levels.

\paragraph{Amplitude table.} Per condition, the population median of the plateau response gives the
30-row amplitude table $R(B,F)$ over six backgrounds, $B=0.01$--$100\,\mu$M; the $B=0$ background is
excluded because it has no foreground step above background, so the amplitude table has six, not
seven, background levels.

\paragraph{Midpoints under an absolute threshold.} For each cell the response is interpolated on the
log concentration axis at a fixed level $0.5$; cells whose maximal response is below $0.5$ are
right-censored. This yields per-background medians of $2.03$, $2.90$, $2.17$, $2.58$, $3.49$,
$13.92$ and $119.56\,\mu$M and censors $179/268$, $69/101$, $35/57$, $38/70$, $40/65$, $46/102$ and
$159/239$ cells, i.e.\ $45$--$68\%$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:main}a, blue; Fig.~\ref{fig:main}d).

\paragraph{Midpoints under a relative threshold.} For each cell the response is interpolated at half
of that cell's own maximal observed response. The same 902 cells give per-background medians of
$0.873$, $0.830$, $1.039$, $1.273$, $1.867$, $11.653$ and $106.52\,\mu$M, with $2/268$ censored at
$B=0$ and none elsewhere (Fig.~\ref{fig:main}a, red; Fig.~\ref{fig:main}d).

\paragraph{Weber behaviour under both conventions.} Both midpoint families are close to affine in the
background, but with different coefficients and different scatter:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Khalf^{\mathrm{abs}}(B) &= 2.29 + 1.17\,B\ \ \mu\mathrm{M}\quad(\max\ \mathrm{dev.}\ 0.10\dex),\\
\Khalf^{\mathrm{rel}}(B) &= 0.91 + 1.06\,B\ \ \mu\mathrm{M}\quad(\max\ \mathrm{dev.}\ 0.05\dex).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:weber}
\end{equation}
The relative-threshold statistic is both closer to unit slope and better described by a line, and it
is computed from $2.7$ times as many cells because almost none are censored. The absolute-threshold
statistic is the one that was previously reported, with the Weber line
$\Khalf=1.17(1.95+B)\,\mu$M as a rounded version of the first relation in Eq.~\eqref{eq:weber}.

\section{The standard model and the joint fit}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Model class}

A homogeneous cluster of $N$ allosterically coupled receptors in two activity states has activity
\begin{equation}
a(L) \;=\; \frac{(1+L/K_A)^{N}}{(1+L/K_A)^{N} + e^{\varepsilon}\,(1+L/K_I)^{N}},
\label{eq:mwc}
\end{equation}
with inactive- and active-state dissociation constants $K_I<K_A$ and free-energy bias
$\varepsilon$. Perfect adaptation retunes $\varepsilon$ through methylation so that the adapted
activity returns to its ligand-independent set point \cite{BarkaiLeibler1997}. The measured step
response on background $B$ is the activity increment from the adapted state,
\begin{equation}
R(B,F) = a_{\max}\,\big[\,a(B+F) - a_{\mathrm{adapted}}(B)\,\big],
\label{eq:response}
\end{equation}
and the model midpoint is defined by $R(B,\Khalf^{\mathrm{model}})=\tfrac12 R(B,\infty)$, that is,
by half of the model's own maximal increment. This is a \emph{relative} definition, and it matches
the relative-threshold measurement of Section~\ref{sec:statistics}, not the absolute one. Solving
Eq.~\eqref{eq:response} gives $\Khalf^{\mathrm{model}}$ in closed form:
\begin{equation}
\Khalf^{\mathrm{model}}(B) \;=\; \frac{Q-1}{1/K_I - Q/K_A},
\qquad
Q=\exp\!\Big[g(B)+\frac{1}{N}\ln\!\frac{1-a^{*}/2}{a^{*}/2}\Big],
\qquad
g(B)=\ln\frac{1+B/K_I}{1+B/K_A},
\label{eq:closed}
\end{equation}
where $a^{*}$ is the adapted activity and the expression returns a concentration on the
\emph{total-ligand} axis $B+F$, the same axis as the measurement.

\subsection{Joint-fit protocol}

We fit Eq.~\eqref{eq:response} jointly to the 30-row amplitude table (least squares, reported as
$R^2$) and to the seven per-background midpoint medians (absolute error in dex), sweeping the weight
$\lambda$ between the two objectives to trace the Pareto front. The acceptance region is the
pre-specified one used previously: $R^2_{\mathrm{amplitude}}>0.9$ and mean
$|\Delta\Khalf|<0.1\dex$. We treat the third clause of the original criterion, $a_{\max}\approx1$,
separately, because it is not satisfiable for this model: the model's maximal response is
$a_{\max}a^{*}=a_{\max}/3$, while the largest measured amplitude is $0.4797$, so $a_{\max}\ge1.44$
is required by the data before any fit is attempted.

Under the corrected protocol a single parameter set, obtained by fitting the amplitude table alone
($\lambda=0$), already predicts the seven relative midpoints with a mean error of $0.023\dex$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:main}c). Increasing the midpoint weight moves the parameters only mildly
($K_I$ from $30.7$ to $21.4\,\mu$M, $N$ from $39$ to $24.6$) and reduces the error to $0.016\dex$,
while amplitude $R^2$ stays at $0.978$. There is no trade-off to resolve: the two statistics are
jointly satisfiable, and the front lies entirely inside the acceptance region.
Fig.~\ref{fig:main}c shows why. Under the absolute convention the residuals at the
amplitude-optimal point are all negative and grow monotonically with background
($-0.33$ to $-1.21\dex$), the signature of a systematic axis displacement. Under the relative
convention the same parameters give residuals between $-0.01$ and $+0.07\dex$, with no trend.

\subsection{The correction also rescues the absolute-threshold statistic}

The axis error is a separate defect from the definition error, and fixing it alone already removes
most of the previous result. With the midpoint still taken at the absolute threshold but reported on
the total-ligand axis, the endpoint error falls from $0.622$ to $0.257\dex$, and a finer sweep of the
weighting finds an acceptable point at $\lambda=3.5$ ($R^2=0.905$, error $0.092\dex$). The original
analysis swept seven values of $\lambda$ and stepped from $3$ ($R^2=0.914$, $0.105\dex$) to $10$
($R^2=0.578$, $0.097\dex$); the crossing lies between them and was not sampled. That fit sits at the
upper parameter bound $N=60$, so it is not comfortable, but it satisfies the stated criterion, and
the claim that \emph{no} parameter set meets the acceptance region is therefore false even on the
absolute-threshold statistic.

\subsection{The cooperativity contradiction dissolves}

The original inference drew a further conclusion: the Weber slope of the midpoints implies
$N\approx2$--$4$, while the amplitude table requires $N\approx12$--$39$, and no single cooperativity
serves both. That inference assumed the two statistics measure the same underlying midpoint. They do
not. The amplitude table and the \emph{relative} midpoints are jointly fit by a single $N=39$; the
relative midpoint law is itself the model's prediction for the background dependence of the
half-maximal concentration, so the two statistics are not independent constraints on $N$ in the way
the contradiction assumed. With the midpoint defined relatively, there is no contradiction to
resolve.

\subsection{A prospective test that stands}

One result of the earlier analysis is unaffected by the correction, because it concerns an empirical
description of the amplitude table rather than the model. The amplitude table is described by a
four-parameter kernel
\begin{equation}
R(F,B) \;=\; A\,\frac{\ln\!\big(1+F/K_c\big)}{1+(B/B_s)^{p}},
\qquad
A=0.2268,\ K_c=0.570\,\mu\mathrm{M},\ B_s=69.7\,\mu\mathrm{M},\ p=0.778,
\label{eq:g10}
\end{equation}
with $R^2=0.967$. Predictions for two mixed-ligand arms were frozen on 2026-08-16, before the
corresponding files were downloaded, using Eq.~\eqref{eq:g10}, the cross-ligand potency ratio
$\rho=23$, and the absolute-threshold Weber line. The midpoint clauses hit: arm V1
($B_{\mathrm{eff}}=330\,\mu$M), predicted $388\,\mu$M, measured median $401.3\,\mu$M
(IQR 388.5--407.6), $+0.014\dex$; arm V2, predicted $122\,\mu$M, measured $133.2\,\mu$M
(IQR 122.8--140.4), $+0.038\dex$; the ambiguity control V2\_alt, $+0.031\dex$ (tolerance
$\pm0.15\dex$). The amplitude clause missed (maximum deviations $0.105$ and $0.126$ against a
tolerance of $0.08$), and the structural clause was unreachable in the compressed coordinate window.
This is a validated empirical extrapolation for the absolute-threshold midpoint; it does not
discriminate between model classes.

\subsection{The fitted cooperativity is not identified}
\label{sec:profile}

Fixing $N$ and refitting the remaining three parameters gives the profile of Fig.~\ref{fig:profile}a.
The amplitude fit rises steeply up to $N\approx15$ and is flat thereafter: $R^2=0.935$ at $N=8$,
$0.970$ at $N=12$, $0.977$ at $N=15$, $0.979$ at $N=20$, and $0.980$ at $N=39$ and at $N=60$. The
acceptance region is entered already at $N=8$, but only with $K_A$ at its upper bound
($2\times10^{4}\,\mu$M, the irreversible-binding limit): $K_A$ stays pinned at that bound for every
$N\le15$, and falls to a finite value only once $N$ exceeds about 20 ($440\,\mu$M at $N=20$,
$132\,\mu$M at $N=39$, $96\,\mu$M at $N=60$). Cooperativity and active-state affinity are therefore
traded along a ridge, and this archive constrains the combination rather than $N$ alone. What the
data support is a lower bound, $N\gtrsim8$; the value $N=39$ reported by the joint fit is one point
on the ridge and should not be read as a measurement of cooperativity.

\subsection{What the single-parameter description leaves out}
\label{sec:diversity}

The model returns one midpoint per background, while the cells return a distribution
(Fig.~\ref{fig:profile}b). Requiring saturation (response at the last stimulus level at least $95\%$
of that cell's maximum, which holds for $579$ of $902$ cells) so that the spread is not truncation,
the within-background standard deviation of $\log_{10}\Khalf$ is $0.26\dex$ at $B=0$, $0.22$, $0.17$,
$0.15$ and $0.11\dex$ for $B=0.01$--$1\,\mu$M, and $0.02$--$0.03\dex$ at $B=10$ and $100\,\mu$M.
The population is therefore dispersed by a factor of about $1.3$ at low background and is nearly
uniform at high background.

The dispersion is not random with respect to the cells' gain. Centring $\log_{10}\Khalf$ within
each background and ranking against the per-cell maximal response gives a Spearman correlation of
$\rho=+0.205$ ($p=7\times10^{-7}$, $n=579$; Fig.~\ref{fig:profile}c): cells that respond weakly reach
their own half-maximum at systematically lower concentration than cells that respond strongly. The
pooled difference in median midpoint between the weak and the strong half is $0.02\dex$, but it is
background dependent and reaches $0.19\dex$ at $B=1\,\mu$M. Two caveats attach to this number. The
dose--response is shallow at low background, so part of the apparent spread there is noise rather
than cell-to-cell variation, and the archive contains no replicates with which to separate the two.
Against that, the sign of the correlation is opposite to the one a noise artefact would produce
(noise inflates the apparent maximum of weakly responding cells and would push their midpoint up),
so a real component is indicated; its size, however, is small. This is the structure a
single-parameter-set model cannot represent, and it is the direction in which the source study's
report of adaptive sensory diversity is to be sought.

\section{Discussion}

The receptor layer is not ruptured by this archive. With the model midpoint expressed on the same
axis as the measurement and defined against the same reference level, one parameter set of the
standard MWC class with perfect adaptation reproduces both the 30-row amplitude table
($R^2=0.980$) and the seven per-background midpoints (mean error $0.023\dex$), across four decades
of background. The previously reported $0.62\dex$ incompatibility is accounted for by two
conventions: a foreground-versus-total concentration axis, which contributes most of the error, and
an absolute-versus-relative midpoint threshold.

The result is a caution rather than a triumph for the model. What has been shown is adequacy for the
population medians, and adequacy is all: the cooperativity is not identified (Section~\ref{sec:profile}),
so no physical claim about cluster size follows from this fit, in either direction. The
absolute-threshold statistic, although a legitimate quantity, requires a fit pinned at the upper
parameter bound and censors up to $68\%$ of cells, so conclusions drawn from it alone are fragile.
The empirical kernel \eqref{eq:g10} predicts unseen mixed-ligand arms to $0.014$--$0.038\dex$ while
the amplitude clause fails, which makes it a better description of the concentration axis than of the
response magnitude. And the population itself carries structure that a single parameter set cannot
hold: a factor of about $1.3$ in midpoint dispersion at low background, with a weak but robust
sensitivity--gain correlation (Section~\ref{sec:diversity}).

The methodological lesson is concrete and generalisable. A midpoint is not a property of a dataset
until its reference level is stated, and the two natural conventions differ here by up to $0.5\dex$
and by a factor of $2.7$ in usable cells. Where a model is compared against midpoints, the
comparison is only meaningful if the model's midpoint and the measurement's midpoint are defined
against the same reference and reported on the same axis. In this case, an unstated convention was
sufficient to reverse a published conclusion about whether a standard model class works.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Data and statistics construction}

The archive of Moore \emph{et al.} \cite{Moore2024} (Dryad doi:10.5061/dryad.nvx0k6dzz, CC0) was
used as released. Plateau responses were extracted per cell and condition as the difference between
the pre-stimulus baseline (last four samples) and the stimulus plateau (last six samples) of the
median response across repeats at each stimulus level, requiring at least four repeats per level and
three usable levels per cell. The amplitude table $R(B,F)$ collects population medians across the 30
background-by-foreground units. Per-cell midpoints were interpolated on the log total-concentration
axis under each of the two reference levels of Section~\ref{sec:statistics}.

\subsection{Model fitting}

Parameters $(K_I,K_A,N,a_{\max})$ were fitted in log space by multi-start least squares, with the
weight $\lambda$ swept over $\{0,0.1,0.3,1,3,6,10,20,30\}$ and, for the axis-only protocol, over
$\{2,2.5,3,3.5,4,4.5,5,5.5,6,7\}$. Model midpoints were evaluated with the closed form of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:closed}, verified against the numerical root of Eq.~\eqref{eq:response} to
$1.1\times10^{-12}$ relative accuracy. The midpoint was reported on the total-ligand axis; reporting
the foreground increment instead is the axis error identified here.

The profile in $N$ was obtained by fixing $N$ at each of
$\{3,5,8,10,12,15,20,25,30,39,50,60\}$ and refitting the other three parameters under the same
bounds. A per-cell midpoint enters the dispersion analysis only when the response at the last
stimulus level reaches at least $95\%$ of that cell's maximum, and only when the half-maximum
crossing lies inside the pair of stimulus levels that brackets it; crossings that would require
extrapolation beyond the bracket (three cells at $B=0$, two at $B=100\,\mu$M) were discarded as
numerically undefined.

\subsection{Reproducibility}

The analysis scripts are at github.com/lihuanlz/chemotaxis-receptor-layer, archived at Zenodo
(doi:10.5281/zenodo.23402856; umbrella edition: doi:10.5281/zenodo.23008824). The corrected protocol
of this paper, including both midpoint definitions and the closed-form model midpoint, is distributed
with the manuscript as \texttt{recheck\_chemotaxis.py}, and regenerates the 30-row amplitude table,
both midpoint tables, the censoring counts, and every entry of Table~\ref{tab:front}. The profile of
Fig.~\ref{fig:profile}a and the dispersion analysis of Fig.~\ref{fig:profile}b,c regenerate from
\texttt{deep\_N\_diversity.py} and \texttt{fig2\_v3.py}.
